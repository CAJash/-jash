Duration & Convexity — Lecture Notes

Duration measures a bond's sensitivity to interest rate changes. Modified Duration approximates the % price change for a 1% yield change. Macaulay Duration is the weighted-average time to receive cash flows.

Convexity accounts for the curvature in the price-yield relationship. Positively convex bonds gain more when rates fall than they lose when rates rise.

⚡ Key Exam Points

  • Modified Duration = Macaulay Duration ÷ (1 + y/m)
  • Higher duration = higher interest rate risk
  • Zero-coupon bonds: Macaulay Duration = Maturity
  • Callable bonds show negative convexity at low yields
  • ΔP/P ≈ −D × Δy + ½ × Convexity × (Δy)²

QUESTION 09 · EQUITY VALUATION
A company has an expected dividend of $2.50 next year, a required return of 10%, and a sustainable growth rate of 4%. Using the Gordon Growth Model, what is the intrinsic value of the stock?
A$25.00
B$31.25
C$41.67
D$62.50

✅ Correct Answer: C — $41.67

Gordon Growth Model: P₀ = D₁ ÷ (r − g) = $2.50 ÷ (0.10 − 0.04) = $2.50 ÷ 0.06 = $41.67

Remember: required return (r) must always exceed growth rate (g), otherwise the model breaks down.
